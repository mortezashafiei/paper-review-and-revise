# Publisher guide: official sites, guidelines and templates

Use this guide for any venue that has no profile (section 13.1 of `SKILL.md`). For each large publisher it gives the official domains, where each journal's instructions live, and which templates exist. Publisher-wide rules are only a starting point: every journal can add its own, so always read the journal's own page. The links were checked on 2026-11-17; if one has moved, search within the publisher's own site rather than elsewhere.

## Checking that a journal is genuine

- **[Think. Check. Submit.](https://thinkchecksubmit.org/journals/)**: a checklist for judging whether a journal can be trusted.
- **[Retraction Watch Hijacked Journal Checker](https://retractionwatch.com/the-retraction-watch-hijacked-journal-checker/)**: known cases of fake websites that copy a real journal's title and ISSN.
- **DOAJ (doaj.org)**: the Directory of Open Access Journals, which lists vetted open-access journals.
- **The domain itself.** The journal's page should sit on its publisher's official domain (the second column above). A look-alike domain is a warning sign.

## Templates in general

- Take templates from the venue's own page or from a link on it. Overleaf's gallery (overleaf.com/latex/templates) also holds templates uploaded by individuals; use only those the publisher marks as official or links to itself.
- Where a venue's written guidelines and its template disagree, the written guidelines win.
- Many venues accept a free-format first submission and apply their template only after acceptance. When the guidelines say so, the template is optional at first submission.

## Conferences

Conference websites change with every edition. Start from the organizing society (IEEE, ACM, ASCE or ASME above, for example), find the current edition, and read its call for papers and author kit. Never rely on the dates, page limits or templates of a past edition.
